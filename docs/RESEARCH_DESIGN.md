# Research design

The review brings household-economic theory together with behavioural explanations for saving, consumption and financial risk-taking during recessions. It distinguishes precautionary motives from the resources available to act on those motives.

Income persistence, liquid assets, debt, credit access and policy support condition observed responses. Changes in portfolio holdings can reflect prices, beliefs, constraints and preferences, so they are not interpreted as direct measures of changing risk preferences.

The United States, Greece and India provide analytical contrasts rather than a controlled experiment. The review is narrative and purposively selected. A future empirical study would require longitudinal household evidence, consistent measurements and explicit identifying assumptions.

The full manuscript and archived-appendix reproduction are linked from this repository's README. The public paper uses source-specific definitions and vintages and does not pool them into a single cross-country saving rate.
